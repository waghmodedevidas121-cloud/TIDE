1.  TIDE (REVISED) -- BATHYMETRY
================================================================================

  EXPLOITS
    Superlinear reward in exposure + the fact that contour-following your own
    frontier is the real skill.
    The source analysis showed the genuine core skill is tracing your own
    border to minimise exposure. A map whose shape decides every route makes
    that skill the entire game.

  CORE MECHANIC
    A designed archipelago. Shallow reef flats are impassable sand. Deep
    channels are always open water. You cannot enclose across a sandbank --
    so THE SHAPE OF THE SEABED DICTATES EVERY ROUTE.

  Resource : Navigable water -- effectively, time-to-bank.
  Risk     : Routing. Long open crossings and chokepoints between rivals.
  Reward   : Contiguous, defensible area in a map you chose to fight over.

  WHY IT IS THE STRONGEST
    The special thing IS the map. Every other concept adds a second system on
    top of the enclosure loop. This one adds a CONSTRAINT to the terrain.
    Nothing else to learn.

  KILL-TEST
    Can a player name a good route within 30 seconds of spawning?
    If not, the map carries no weight and this collapses.


================================================================================
2.  QUARRY -- THE SCAR
================================================================================

  EXPLOITS
    Death erases 100% of territory, which means the map is ALREADY a
    renewable, decaying resource. The analysis found erasing is the single
    most consequential thing in the game.
    Quarry makes YOU the eraser.

  CORE MECHANIC
    Enclosed ground is claimed AND permanently excavated. The crater is
    impassable and never comes back.

  Resource : Neutral map -- finite, and you are spending it.
  Risk     : Attrition. Hoarding map is hoarding your own future options.
  Reward   : A map progressively denied to your enemies.
             AGGRESSION BECOMES PROVABLE.

  WHY IT IS MEMORABLE
    The arena becomes a record of the match. You can point at a crater and
    say who made it. No .io game has a scar.
    Craters on a flat plane are the single most screenshot-able territory
    visual in the genre.

  KILL-TEST
    Does a 40% player ruin the round for everyone within 60 seconds?
    If yes, dead.
    (See the verdict. This is what kills it.)


================================================================================
3.  LATTICE -- CONNECTIVITY
================================================================================

  EXPLOITS
    Territory is scored by raw area, so the optimal shape is a boring
    ribbon, and the genuine contour skill goes unrewarded.
    Lattice makes TOPOLOGY the constraint instead.

  CORE MECHANIC
    Territory scores ONLY if it is one connected region.
    Split it and the smaller piece instantly zeroes.

  Resource : Graph integrity.
  Risk     : Self-fragmentation. Expanding can REDUCE your score.
  Reward   : Connected, defensible area that compounds into structures.

  WHY IT IS MEMORABLE
    Wireframes. Circuits, mycelium, neural nets.
    Nobody else in .io produces this silhouette, and it reads as a different
    genre entirely.

  KILL-TEST
    Do players understand "only your biggest piece counts" BEFORE their first
    split? If they split and do not know why, it feels like a bug and they
    leave.


================================================================================
4.  SHADOW -- THE LANTERN
================================================================================

  EXPLOITS
    Every threat in the genre is anonymous -- a leaderboard bar, a clock, a
    score going down. Nothing has a face.
    Shadow replaces every timer with A VISIBLE ENEMY THAT HAS A POSITION.

  CORE MECHANIC
    A roaming light source crosses the arena. Ground it touches cannot be
    claimed. Close a loop in darkness and it is yours.

  Resource : Safe unlit ground.
  Risk     : A visible hunter you can see coming and probably cannot outrun.
  Reward   : Shadow area, plus the satisfaction of out-planning something
              with intent.

  WHY IT IS MEMORABLE
    Every screenshot has a CHARACTER in it -- a shape with a position and a
    direction.
    It converts anonymous pressure into a specific enemy, which is the
    cleanest possible fix for the "the game took my points" blame problem.

  KILL-TEST
    Is the hunter predictable enough to play against but unpredictable enough
    to be frightening?
    Predictable  -> it is scenery.
    Unpredictable-> it is unfair.
    This balance is the whole project.


================================================================================
5.  FORGE -- THE COOLING LAND
================================================================================

  EXPLOITS
    Zero-maintenance territory is load-bearing.
    The analysis proved permanent assets are what make greed correct.
    Forge converts it to a MAINTAINED asset -- the exact change TIDE was
    reaching for, but without a clock.

  CORE MECHANIC
    Freshly captured territory is white-hot and cools.
    Cold land returns to neutral.
    You re-heat it by sailing over it.

  Resource : Heat -- regenerates only while moving.
  Risk     : A maintenance tax. Movement spent defending is movement not
              spent expanding.
  Reward   : Held area, plus a heat bonus on land you just banked.

  WHY IT IS MEMORABLE
    Your land GLOWS AND FADES, and you can watch a rival's territory going
    grey from across the map.
    Critically, every loss is traceable to a choice YOU made. Nothing is ever
    taken by a clock you did not author.

  KILL-TEST
    Do players ever choose to maintain over expanding?
    If they never do, it is decoration and must be cut.

WHY THE SCORES READ THAT WAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  REPLAYABILITY
    Lattice and Forge top out at 4 because routing stays fresh longest.
    Quarry and Shadow score 4 on genuinely emergent variation.
    TIDE scores 3 because route choice is a narrower decision set than the
    others.

  RETENTION
    Forge and Lattice score 5. Both create a genuine TWO-WAY decision
    (expand vs. maintain / expand vs. stay connected) that survives hundreds
    of matches. Quarry and TIDE score 3 because the strategic question is
    narrower. Shadow scores 3 because its depth depends entirely on AI
    quality.

  SIMPLICITY
    TIDE scores 5 and it is not close. "Shallow is sand, deep is water" is
    the whole tutorial.
    Lattice scores 2 because the rule is one sentence but ABSTRACT, and its
    failure is invisible until it costs you everything.

  VIRAL POTENTIAL
    Quarry scores 5. Craters on a flat plane are the most screenshot-able
    territory visual in the genre, and nobody looks like it.
    Lattice scores 2. Wireframes are distinctive but hard to explain in a
    still image.

  DEVELOPMENT COST
    TIDE, Forge and Quarry sit at 3. Each is roughly one extra array plus one
    extra rule. Lattice and Shadow score 2. Lattice needs connected-component
    labelling per player per capture; Shadow needs a roaming AI plus a
    lighting layer.

RECOMMENDATION:  TIDE (REVISED) -- BATHYMETRY
================================================================================

  ONE concept. Not a portfolio.


THE ARGUMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. ITS STRENGTH IS THAT ITS SPECIAL THING IS A CONSTRAINT, NOT A SYSTEM.
     Every rival adds machinery -- a heat economy, a lighting layer, a
     connectivity solver, a crater system.
     TIDE adds TERRAIN.
     There is nothing else to teach, nothing else to balance, nothing else
     to get wrong.
     That is why it scores 5 on simplicity while scoring 4 or below on
     everything else.


  2. IT IS THE ONLY ONE OF THE FIVE THAT A SINGLE DOMINANT PLAYER CANNOT
     BREAK.
     Quarry is the joint-highest scorer and it is the one I am rejecting.
     The reason is exactly its strength:

         IT ACCUMULATES, AND ROUNDS DO NOT HAVE TIME.

     In a 3-minute round, once someone hits 40% they have excavated 40% of
     the arena into unusable rubble, and the remaining 60% is fragmented
     scraps. Everyone else is now playing a dead map.
     By the third match of a 10-minute session the arena is unplayable.
     That is not a tuning problem. That is arithmetic.


  3. THE MAP RESETS EVERY ROUND, BY CONSTRUCTION.
     Bathymetry is fixed at match start.
     Every round gets a fresh, clean, deliberately designed arena -- which
     is precisely what a short-round game needs and precisely what an
     accumulating mechanic cannot provide.


  4. IT IS THE CHEAPEST THING TO VALIDATE.
     Map generation and one passability check are the entire delta.
     The kill-test is answerable in a single playtest:
     can a player name a route in 30 seconds?


  5. IT OWNS THE ONLY UNOCCUPIED SPACE IN THE COMPETITIVE SET.
     No game in the research has a designed map as its primary strategic
     object.
     Paper.io's arena is a blank square.
     Paper.io 2's is a blank circle.
     Hexanaut's is hexagons plus five totems.
     That gap will not stay open.


RUNNER-UP, AND WHY IT LOST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  FORGE.

  On the pure decision depth it is the better GAME. Its expand-vs-maintain
  tension is the strongest single decision in this set, and it has the best
  fix for TIDE's original blame problem.

  It loses because it needs a second economy, and a second economy in a
  3-minute browser game is a lot of arithmetic to explain to an eight-year-
  old.

  IF THE BATHYMETRY KILL-TEST FAILS, FORGE IS WHAT I BUILD INSTEAD.
  It is the concept to fall back to.


REJECTED ON STRUCTURAL GROUNDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  QUARRY   -- snowball
  SHADOW   -- binary risk, no middle setting exists
  LATTICE  -- abstract rule whose failure mode reads as a bug
